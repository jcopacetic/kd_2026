When you leave a bundled website vendor, you can usually take your domain and the content you supplied, but not the site's design, and possibly not the phone numbers on your listings. What you keep depends on your contract, so read it first. Then move in this order: build the new site, move the domain or point it at the new site, update your listings, and cancel last. Cancelling first is how businesses end up offline with a phone number that no longer rings.

This applies to Hibu, Thryv and any provider that sells a website together with listings, reviews, SEO or ads for one monthly fee. ## What do you actually own?

Usually the domain (if it's registered to you), the text and photos you supplied, and your Google Business Profile. The design, the platform and anything the vendor licensed typically stay with the vendor.

| Item | Typical position with a bundled vendor | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Domain name | Yours if registered in your name; sometimes registered to the vendor | Who the registrant is (below) |
| Site design and template | Licensed to you while you pay; stays with the vendor | The ownership or intellectual property clause |
| Text and photos you supplied | Yours | Keep your own copies |
| Text the vendor wrote for you | Depends on the contract | Whether it's licensed to you after you leave |
| Stock images | Licensed by the vendor; usually not transferable | Replace them on the new site |
| Google Business Profile | Yours, if you're the primary owner | Your role in the profile |
| Reviews | Live on Google, Facebook and other platforms, not on the vendor's site | That you can log into each platform |
| Call-tracking numbers | Usually the vendor's | Which numbers appear on your listings |
| Form submissions and leads | Stored in the vendor's dashboard | Export them before access ends |

## What do Hibu's and Thryv's terms say?

Both publish their terms, and both have changed them over time. What follows is what the current versions say as of October 5, 2026. Your agreement may be an older version, so check the one you signed.

**Hibu** (Digital Products and Services Terms and Conditions, hibu.com/legal):

- **Term.** A Hibu Smart Site has a 12-month minimum term, and Hibu One a six-month minimum. After that, both renew month to month. You can't cancel within the minimum term.
- **Notice.** Cancellation is by phone. Hibu must receive the request at least 15 days before the end of the minimum term or the current service month; otherwise the service runs one more month.
- **Domain.** If Hibu provides the domain for a Smart Site, the terms say it "will be registered to us," and that Hibu will assist if you ask to transfer it into your name.
- **Content.** Hibu owns the rights in content it creates. If you paid for the service in full in advance, the terms give you a perpetual right to use the written copy Hibu created for you after cancellation.
- **After cancellation.** Hibu says it isn't responsible for storing your data or content, and that backing it up is your responsibility.
- **Call tracking.** The terms say the tracking number is for use on your website only, and that if a service including it isn't paid for, Hibu may disconnect the number or forward calls to it to other advertisers in your market.

**Thryv** (Services Agreement, Rev March 2026, and Professional Website Terms, last updated July 1, 2026):

- **Renewal and notice.** The initial term is set in your order. Digital services then renew for one-month terms unless Thryv receives written cancellation notice at least 30 days before the end of the current term, by mail or through its online contact form.
- **Bundles.** If you cancel part of a bundle, Thryv may charge the full bundle price or charge the remaining items at à la carte rates.
- **Website.** The design and the content Thryv creates or licenses remain Thryv's. On termination, Thryv releases your client content, and your website's address if the domain was yours originally. It doesn't release third-party content it licensed.
- **Call tracking.** A tracking number may be placed in your organic and directory listings as well as your ads. If charges go unpaid, the agreement allows Thryv to disconnect tracking numbers or redirect them to another company.

None of this is unusual for a bundled service. It does mean the order you do things in matters.

## Step 1: Read your contract

Find four things before you call anyone: the end of your minimum term, how renewal works, the notice period and how notice must be given (phone, writing, a form).

Then look for:

- **Early termination.** Can you leave before the term ends, and at what cost? Some agreements don't allow it; others let the vendor bill the rest of the term.
- **Bundle pricing.** Does dropping one service change the price of the others?
- **Ownership.** Search the agreement for "domain", "intellectual property", "content" and "termination".

Put the notice deadline in your calendar. Missing it by a day can mean another month's bill.

## Step 2: Find out who owns the domain

Look up your domain at ICANN's lookup tool (lookup.icann.org) or ask the vendor in writing: "Who is the registrant of my domain, and at which registrar?" If the registrant is the vendor, ask them to transfer it into your name. My [10-minute ownership check](/insights/who-owns-your-website-ownership-check/) covers the lookup step by step.

Two ICANN rules to plan around:

- Your registrar must give you the transfer code (auth code) within five calendar days of asking.
- You can't move a domain to a different registrar within 60 days of changing the registrant's name, organization or email, and registrars may refuse a transfer within 60 days of registration or a previous transfer.

If the vendor stalls, [what to do when someone won't give you your domain](/insights/web-designer-wont-give-domain/) walks through escalation.

## Step 3: Export everything before you cancel

Assume access ends the day the service does. Export:

- [ ] Every page's text (copy it into a document, page by page)
- [ ] Your photos, logo and any video you supplied, at full size
- [ ] Form submissions, leads and contact lists from the vendor dashboard
- [ ] Call recordings or call logs you need
- [ ] Your list of pages and their addresses (for redirects on the new site)
- [ ] Logins for your Google Business Profile, Facebook, Yelp, Bing and Apple listings, and confirmation that you're an owner on each
- [ ] Ad accounts: whether they're in your name and whether you can keep the history
- [ ] Email: if your business email runs through the vendor, set up new mailboxes first

## Why tracking numbers can break your listings

A call-tracking number forwards to your real phone so the vendor can count calls. If the vendor owns it and it stops forwarding when you cancel, every listing showing that number stops ringing.

That matters more than it sounds. Your phone number is part of how Google and directories match your business across the web, and Google's guidelines say a Business Profile phone number "must be under the direct control of the business." Before you leave:

1. Check your Google Business Profile, Bing, Apple, Yelp, Facebook and any directories for numbers you don't recognize.
2. Ask the vendor which numbers are theirs and whether any can be ported (moved) to you.
3. Put your own number back as the primary number everywhere, before cancellation.

## How to move without downtime

Build the new site first and cancel last. In order:

1. **Get the new site ready** at a temporary address, with your content, contact forms tested and redirects from old page addresses mapped.
2. **Secure the domain.** If it's yours, either transfer it to a registrar where you hold the login or change its DNS (the settings that point it at a website) to the new host. If the vendor is the registrant, get it moved into your name first.
3. **Move email** if it's tied to the domain, so mail keeps arriving.
4. **Switch the site live** by pointing the domain at the new host. Check it on a phone.
5. **Update your listings**: website link and your own phone number on Google, Bing, Apple, Facebook, Yelp and directories.
6. **Give notice and cancel**, in the form your contract requires, inside the notice window. Keep the confirmation.
7. **Watch for a month**: calls, form submissions and your Google profile.
